# Engineering review E2 — 8 November 2026

**DESIGN NO-GO (HOLD); PHYSICAL POWER-UP NO-GO (HOLD).** The proposed topology is documented and software-verifiable, but protection assemblies and load-dependent parts are not fully selected. Calling this a final released circuit would conceal material unknowns. This package completes the available desk work and explicitly identifies the remaining evidence. No physical port was opened, battery connected or firmware uploaded. The ESP32's older installed firmware remains unchanged.

## Evidence boundary and changes

Reviewed actual local firmware, controller, GPIO header, PlatformIO, Wokwi, tests, 188 pre-existing connection rows, electrical model, diagrams and audit findings. Preserved prior uncommitted F01–F03 export corrections. The unrelated untracked checklist PNG is excluded from publication. No identifiable hardware photographs or manufacturer labels were found in this repository; the checklist image is not rover evidence. Historical E1 material remains historical and is superseded by E2 for assembly.

E2 adds U2, C_U2, R30–32 and three intermediate divider nets: **87 components, 72 nets, 292 terminal dispositions, 201 connection records**. Four records inspect existing internal bonds; 197 describe external conductors. All terminals have a net or explicit NC disposition. No GPIO, firmware behavior or motor grouping changed. The 201 records are generated into the schematic, CSV, sectional instructions and simplified assembly sheets; matching these artifacts is a consistency check, not component approval.

**Corrected sequencing gap:** sensor V5_LOGIC can remain live while V5_MCU/MCU 3V3 is absent. The old divider alone limited voltage when the ESP32 was powered but did not establish safe voltage on its unpowered input. E2 routes each divider through **TI SN74LVC3G34DCU**, supplied by ESP1.3V3; documented Ioff supports the powered-off state. Front uses input1/output7, left input3/output5, right input6/output2; pin8 VCC and pin4 GND. R30–32 are 100k output biases and C_U2 is 100nF local bypass. This addresses that path at design level; supply ramps, leakage and unknown module paths still require physical qualification. [TI U2 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc3g34.pdf).

## Interface calculations and acceptance

Each ECHO upper/lower divider is 2.2k/3.3k, 1%. Assuming the **actual ECHO HIGH** follows a qualified 4.75–5.25V supply, the divided HIGH is 2.827–3.175V. The assumption must be measured: a generic TTL output description does not guarantee rail-level HIGH. Worst input leakage of 5µA through about 1.32k Thevenin resistance changes the node by about 6.6mV; the lower calculated HIGH remains above U2's 2.0V HIGH threshold at 3.0–3.6V VCC. Confirm input LOW <=0.8V and voltage remains 0–5.5V including overshoot. U2 is not Schmitt-triggered: its input transition limit is <=10ns/V at 3.3V ±0.3V. Place divider and buffer together, keep their node traces short, and scope actual cable edges. If that limit fails, explicitly redesign with a qualified Schmitt interface; do not approve it from a DMM reading. [TI U2 limits](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc3g34.pdf).

The buffer output shares the ESP32 supply. Under <=100µA load, the specified HIGH is at least VDD−0.1V and LOW at most 0.1V; 100k draws at most about 36.4µA at 3.6V with 1% low resistance, leaving margin for verified GPIO leakage. Compare to ESP32 HIGH >=0.75×VDD and LOW <=0.25×VDD. Raw 5V ECHO is never attached to the MCU. GPIO34/35/36 are input-only and appropriate here. The WROOM module itself needs 3.0–3.6V; a DevKit's qualified 5V input is a separate board-level matter. [Espressif ESP32 datasheet](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf).

U1 remains **SN74AHCT244N**, non-inverting at protected logic 5V, VCC20/GND10, OE1/OE19 both LOW. Four PWM channels: 2->18, 4->16, 6->14, 8->12; shared EN 11->9; TRIG channels 13->7, 15->5, 17->3. AHCT accepts 3.3V drive with HIGH >=2V; HC is not an equivalent. R5=2.2k holds GPIO14/U1.11 LOW during the GPIO14 reset bias; seven other U1 input pulls are 10k. Six receiver EN pulls are 10k and draw 3.0mA at 5V, or about 3.182mA at 5.25V with 1% low resistors, before module input loading. U1's specified ±8mA output drive is not permission to ignore summed pull-ups, receiver input currents or output HIGH/LOW thresholds. Remove hard-HIGH links; verify no GPIO/buffer drives against supply links. Scope each EN through reset, brownout and rail-order changes. AHCT input overvoltage tolerance does not imply powered-off output isolation; unknown carrier pull-ups remain a hold. [TI AHCT244 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/SN74AHCT244).

## Power and grounding

BT1 PACK+ -> adjacent F0 -> S0 -> high-side P1 -> protected positive distribution. F1 feeds BUCK1 then OV1 then V5_MCU to the verified DevKit input. F2 feeds BUCK2 then OV2 then V5_LOGIC for sensors/U1/driver logic. **Both buck input branches are before K1.** K1 switches only motor power: FL -> left BTS B+, FR -> right BTS B+, FA -> L298 Vs. Positive buck outputs never join. All returns share a reference with dedicated high-current star paths; no breadboard, USB or GPIO cable carries motor current.

P1 must protect each cell and match the actual pack chemistry, current and balance connection; its CELL0..3 names are potentials, not connector positions. Actual 11.1V/3300mAh values come from user inventory, not a confirmed label. **12.6V maximum is conditional on ordinary 4.2V-per-cell 3S chemistry**, not confirmation of the battery. Manufacturer, chemistry, C rating, charge/discharge limits, connector and condition are missing. No numeric fuse, wire gauge, contactor current or protection threshold is released without those data and motor evidence.

Each LM2596 module must be individually identified and qualified at input range, peak output demand, heat and ripple. The regulator IC's 3A figure does not establish carrier capability or independent OVP. OV1/OV2 must provide output OVP/OCP and fail-open isolated healthy contacts compatible with the coil; they are still functional requirements, not identified commercial boards. USB-only programming requires disconnected external harnesses; mixed USB/buck operation needs an actual DevKit schematic and proven isolation/backfeed solution. A missing VBUS wire alone does not qualify all signal paths. Test both directions of partial powering, including L298 inputs and module pull-ups. [TI LM2596 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lm2596.pdf).

C_BL/BR/BA reservoirs, Z_L/R/A clamps and coil suppression are explicit selection positions. Choose standoff, clamp voltage, ESR/ripple/energy and fuse coordination from actual maximum normal pack voltage, module limits and measured regeneration. Assess simultaneous braking and K1 opening with stored motor/bus energy; verify discharge and stopping time. A TVS cannot be assumed to absorb sustained braking energy. Confirm diode paths and motor noise suppression; never fit one ordinary diode across a reversible motor. Secure batteries and guard rotating mechanisms; no chassis motor return.

## Drivetrain and cleaning qualification

Each BTS module needs B+, B−, M+, M−, VCC, GND, RPWM, LPWM, R_EN, L_EN and separate insulated NC R_IS/L_IS. Verify actual header orientation and logic schematic. B− takes the heavy return; verify the internal GND bond instead of adding a thin parallel return. Two left motors share M+/M−, two right motors share the other module. Each motor A lead has its own FM fuse. Determine physical polarity under later guarded, current-limited testing; a wire color proves nothing. Running, starting, coast/braking and simultaneous stall demand must fit the **whole board's** thermal, connector, copper and chip limits. No driver-current telemetry is wired. [Infineon BTS7960 chip datasheet](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/57/infineon-bts7960-ds-en.pdf).

The active conditional cleaning topology remains **one L298N**: OUT1/2 parallel brushes M5/M6, OUT3/4 conveyor M7; GPIO21/22 IN1/2, GPIO23/25 IN3/4; ENA/ENB share the gated 5V EN. Verify exact carrier, regulator jumper configuration, external Vss supply, local bypassing, sense paths and all recirculation diodes. Both brushes' currents add on A; total heat includes B simultaneously. Delivered voltage is supply minus bridge loss. Conveyor voltage is unknown. A shortage cannot be repaired by a software duty cap. The conditional MOSFET replacement selection/wiring is in [purchase list](FINAL_PURCHASE_LIST.md); it is not incorporated without evidence. [ST L298 datasheet](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/l298.pdf).

Required qualification inequalities: each side's summed run current <= independently established derated board limit; start/stall pulse and I²t within device/board/motor limits for a guaranteed cutoff time; motor RMS current and delivered voltage within its ratings; simultaneous cleaning junction estimate `Tambient + Ptotal * Rtheta_total` below the datasheet operating limit with stated design margin; all pigtails/contacts/wires remain within temperature and drop budgets. Protective clearing must precede the shortest allowable motor/driver/conductor overload time. A lead fuse alone is not guaranteed jam or semiconductor protection. For each motor record manufacturer voltage tolerance, load/start/stall profile and allowed duration; use manufacturer data or a supervised instrumented low-energy method, never a hand-held battery stall.

## Physical emergency stop and restart

FK -> OV1 healthy NO -> OV2 healthy NO -> S2 coil NC -> (S3 NO parallel K1 hold NO) -> K1 coil -> GND. K1 main NO removes all three motor branches; S2's separate EN NC also interrupts buffer9-to-driver enables independently of software. Local pulls keep each disabled. S2's third NC and a separate K1 sense NO return ESP32 3V3 to GPIO27 with external R29=10k/internal pull-down. LOW is unsafe; do not replace this with a pull-up circuit.

With K1 open at boot, firmware intentionally latches ESTOP. Later, in a qualified guarded procedure: verify disabled EN, power-arm with S3, verify HIGH feedback, send RESET (stays disarmed), ARM (stays stopped), then explicit motion. Opening S2 drops coil, enables and feedback; releasing it with S3 released does not reseal K1. Serial ESTOP/STOP latches software disable but does **not** open the contactor or isolate a live motor bus. Stored energy and moving inertia remain. A held/stuck S3, welded contacts or shorted feedback are not fault-tolerantly handled by this simple circuit. Do not claim a safety category; if the risk assessment requires monitored reset/weld detection, select that implementation and revise the model before release. K1 coil range, independent auxiliaries, DC interruption, wetting current and clamp/release time remain HOLD.

## Firmware and simulation review

Existing controller implements disabled startup, ARM, forward/reverse/turn requests, AUTO, side/front clearance, 180ms settle, 100ms neutral, 540ms enabled turn, fresh three-sensor reassessment and maximum three attempts. Cleaning requests are independent but outputs operate **only during actual forward motion**, including AUTO. This is preserved behavior, not stationary cleaning. Commands, sensors and diagnostics are in the operating guide. Manual motion is open loop with a 1200ms command timeout; sensor-fault disarming applies to AUTO. No rear/cliff/current sensing exists.

The GPIO27 falling ISR clears GPIO14 while a ping can block; physical contact interruption remains independent. Main-loop permission uses the same critical section. Serial parsing is bounded; oversized lines disarm. Sensor pings are staggered every 65ms (each sensor nominally 195ms), with 25ms bounded echo wait and 500ms freshness. Invalid/stale AUTO data disarms; recovery alone does not restart. The sensor reference requires a >=10µs trigger and recommends >60ms measurement spacing; actual crosstalk remains untested. [ElecFreaks HC-SR04 reference hosted by SparkFun](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/datasheets/Sensors/Proximity/HCSR04.pdf).

No demonstrable firmware logic bug required a rewrite. New host adapter regressions exercise physical-stop input during both cleaning channels, software reconstruction after restart, and sensor recovery/re-arm. Host tests execute the firmware adapter with Arduino stubs; they do not emulate brownout waveforms, contactors, power electronics or mechanics. Both configured ESP32 targets must compile; the unconfigured Arduino3 API branch is not separately qualified. Wokwi retains equivalent non-inverting digital signal paths on identical GPIOs, with ideal direct echoes/LEDs; it does not model U1/U2 electronics. See [23-case simulation procedure](SIMULATION_VERIFICATION.md) and the fresh evidence report.

## Measurements needed to release the design

1. Identify exact pack, all motors (especially conveyor voltage), DevKit, driver/buck/sensor revisions and all manufacturer terminal maps. Obtain motor start/stall limits, allowed duration and board continuous/thermal evidence.
2. Finish P1, OV1/2, K1/S0/S2/S3, all fuse/wire/connector, capacitor/clamp and overload-protection selections using that evidence. Replace every generic functional safety port with the selected manufacturer's verified terminal map.
3. Unpowered continuity/polarity/insulation and internal bonds; independent current-limited buck and OVP qualification with loads disconnected; sensor raw/divided/U2 output waveforms and off-rail leakage; reset/brownout EN observations.
4. Cutoff/manual reset/dropout/residual-bus tests using a safe test load, then individually qualified motor directions, pair currents, cleaning thermal/jam profiles and regeneration. Record instruments, conditions, traces, limits, reviewer/date. No battery first trial.
5. Upload only with a cable and user-authorized later commissioning; confirm installed firmware identity. Prove sensing and stop distances with guarded low-speed trials before unsupervised AUTO.

Until these gates close, diagrams are complete at represented functional-terminal level and remain **unreleased for physical assembly/power**. Selection holds are not cured by successful compile, model checks or PDF validation.
